Profile

I am an electronics undergraduate on a diploma-to-degree pathway, working toward semiconductor nanofabrication and process integration. My fabrication grounding comes from four programmes - SPARC NanoX at IIT Hyderabad, the Semiconductor Nanofabrication Summer School at CeNSE, IISc Bengaluru, Semiconductor Fabrication 101 from Purdue / UT Austin / Intel, and Duke's Nanotechnology: A Maker's Course - plus hands-on electrical characterization of optoelectronic assemblies across three internships.

Because a fab is not yet available to me, my method is to model the process instead: implement the classical models for oxidation, implantation, diffusion and pattern transfer, benchmark them against known results, and treat the simulator as a way to reason about the physics a real line runs on. Alongside that I work on the data side of manufacturing - statistical process control, yield analytics and ML - where the same instinct applies: find the signal, then say how confident you are in it. Every project carries a limitations list: the modules that are incomplete, the figures that come from public datasets rather than a fab, and the validation still to be done.

AttoFab

Open-source semiconductor process simulator

An education-first fabrication simulator built on classical, citable textbook physics: thermal oxidation (Deal-Grove, with (111)/(100) orientation dependence), Gaussian ion implantation, Fick's-law diffusion, LOCOS-style masked oxidation, mask expose/develop, and cellular-automaton anisotropic and isotropic etch/deposition. The 1D and 2D engines are complete; a hand-rolled GDSII parser and 3D wafer mesh work, with integration still partial. Ships with a physics-regression bench, a FastAPI backend, two web frontends and a CLI.

Scope & limitations Lithography is limited to mask expose/develop; 3D lithography is not implemented. Oxidation substitutes material in place rather than modelling volume expansion, so oxide growth alone creates no topography - only etch does. The Pearson-IV implant model is gated off until its moment-matching is correct, and some 3D flows still run only through a Rust example.
